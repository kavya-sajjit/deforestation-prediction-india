# Model card: `bcos_rgbn_hist` (forest-loss forecaster)

Format follows Mitchell et al. (2019), *Model Cards for Model Reporting*.

## Model details

| | |
|---|---|
| **Developer** | Kavya Sajjit (D.J. Sanghvi College of Engineering, Mumbai) |
| **Version** | v2 (Nov 2026). v1 is retired; see "What changed from v1" in the README |
| **Architecture** | B-cos ResNet-18 (official B-cos v2 code, Böhle et al. 2022/2024), trained from scratch |
| **Input** | 64 × 64 px Sentinel-2 L2A patch (640 m), early-2019 dry-season median composite, bands R, G, B, NIR encoded as `[x, 1−x]` (8 channels), plus past-loss channels (Hansen loss 2001–2018) |
| **Output** | Two logits: *loss* vs *no loss* of ≥2% of the patch's forest in 2020–2023. Calibrated to a probability by `deforest.risk_levels` |
| **Explanation** | Exact contribution map `W(x) ⊙ e` that sums to the logit difference (no bias terms). Checked by a unit test for every variant |
| **Licence** | MIT (code). Sentinel-2 data: Copernicus open licence. Hansen GFC: CC BY 4.0 |

## Intended use

- **Primary use:** rank forest patches by risk so that people monitoring forests (NGOs, forest departments, researchers) can decide where to look first, for example the top 10% riskiest patches.
- **Users:** researchers and monitoring organisations who can check the forecast on the ground.
- **Out of scope:** legal or enforcement decisions, accusing any person or company, estimating carbon, or use outside the four study biomes without first re-running the leave-one-region-out evaluation. Every alert the website produces says it is a statistical forecast and asks for a field check.

## Training data

- **Imagery:** Sentinel-2 L2A from Microsoft Planetary Computer. Clouds and shadows are masked with the Scene Classification Layer, and the processing-baseline offset after 25 Jan 2022 is removed.
- **Labels:** Hansen et al. Global Forest Change v1.11, an independent product, not computed from the input imagery.
- **Regions:** four 30 km × 30 km boxes: Western Ghats (Kodagu–Wayanad), Odisha (Keonjhar), Mizoram, and Central India (Balaghat). Boxes are in `deforest/config.py`.
- **Filtering:** patches must be ≥30% forest at the start of 2019. Patches with 0–2% target-period loss are excluded as ambiguous. Patches with >0.5% loss in 2019 are excluded from forecasting, because the input composite may already show it.

## Evaluation

- **Splits:** 5-fold spatial block cross-validation (5.12 km blocks, one-patch buffer, leakage asserted before every run) and leave-one-region-out.
- **Metrics:** average precision against its chance level (the positive rate), F1, MCC, balanced accuracy, and *loss captured in the top-10% risk* (chance = 10%). Mean ± SD across folds, and 95% block-bootstrap CIs.
- **Baselines:** logistic regression on loss history (`history_lr`) and gradient-boosted trees on hand-made spectral features (`gbm`), on identical splits.
- **Explanation checks:** completeness error, localisation against Hansen loss pixels (compared with Grad-CAM on a standard CNN), the randomisation sanity check (Adebayo et al. 2018), and per-band reliance per region.
- **Results:** generated by `python -m deforest.experiments` and pasted into the README by `scripts/update_readme.py`. *Not run yet as of this version of the card. Update this line with the headline AP and top-10% capture once they exist.*

## Limitations and risks

- **Labels are Hansen's definition of loss**, not field truth: they include rotational shifting cultivation and plantation harvest, and they miss slow degradation. In Mizoram especially, "loss" often means jhum cycles, not permanent clearing.
- **Small geographic sample:** four regions do not represent India. Leave-one-region-out shows how far the model transfers.
- **One forecast date** (early 2019). Known drivers such as roads, settlements, protected-area status and mining leases are not inputs.
- **Patch-level output** (640 m) cannot point to a specific hectare.
- **Forecast explanations show association, not cause:** evidence that lies "where loss later occurs" is not a mechanism.
- **Misuse risk:** a risk map could be read as an accusation against communities living in high-risk areas. Outputs should go to monitoring organisations with the caveats above, not be published as blame.
